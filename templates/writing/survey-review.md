# Survey Writing Mode

Use this built-in mode for survey and review papers with:

- broad but explicit coverage
- durable included/excluded screening packets
- thematic synthesis rather than experiment-first storytelling

## Required Narrative Contract

- Introduction must define the survey scope, why the area matters, and what the reader will get.
- Scope and protocol must make inclusion/exclusion logic explicit.
- Taxonomy must organize the field into defensible families or comparison axes.
- Evidence synthesis must compare clusters, not list papers one by one.
- Benchmark landscape must separate datasets, metrics, and evaluation habits clearly.
- Open problems must be supported by the survey packet, not invented as filler.

## Target Sections

1. Abstract
2. Introduction
3. Scope And Protocol
4. Taxonomy
5. Evidence Synthesis
6. Benchmark Landscape
7. Open Problems
8. Conclusion

## Budget Guidance

- Abstract: `180-260` words
- Introduction: `1.0-1.4` pages
- Scope And Protocol: `0.8-1.2` pages
- Taxonomy: `1.5-2.5` pages
- Evidence Synthesis: `2.0-3.0` pages
- Benchmark Landscape: `1.0-1.8` pages
- Open Problems + Conclusion: `0.8-1.4` pages total

## Storyline Guardrails

- Every major theme must map to representative papers or survey packet evidence.
- Keep contradiction areas and missing coverage explicit.
- Do not flatten benchmark clusters into generic "better/worse" prose.
- The survey brief, coverage summary, and gap synthesis should stay visible in the story contract.

## Citation Guardrails

- Every thematic contrast needs real citations.
- Survey claims about popularity, coverage, or benchmarks must point to explicit included papers or matrices.
- `refs.bib` must be built from source-of-truth metadata, never invented.
