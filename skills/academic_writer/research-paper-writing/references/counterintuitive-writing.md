# Counterintuitive Writing Rules

## Rule 1: Shrink the claim before you strengthen the wording
If the evidence is only strong for a narrow scope, narrow the scope first. A smaller, defense-ready claim beats a bigger, fragile contribution.

## Rule 2: Write the challenge so the method feels necessary
Do not present the method as a clever add-on. Present the challenge so the reader feels that a method with this shape had to appear.

## Rule 3: Remove easy-but-weak contribution framing
If a contribution sounds exciting but collapses under one skeptical reviewer question, demote or delete it before it infects the abstract and introduction.

## Rule 4: Prefer defense-ready contributions over exciting but unsupported ones
The winning paper story is not the one with the largest promise. It is the one that survives novelty attack, unsupported-claim audit, and limitation audit together.

## Rule 5: Do not let related work dissolve the gap
Related work should sharpen the missing piece, not flatten the paper into “another variant in a crowded area.”

## Rule 6: Let limitations increase trust
Limitations are not apology text. They are credibility anchors that tell the reviewer you know the boundary of your actual contribution.

## Rule 7: Optimize for skeptical reviewer reading order
Assume the reviewer looks at title, abstract, intro contribution bullets, main table, and figures first. Put the most defensible version of the story on that path.
