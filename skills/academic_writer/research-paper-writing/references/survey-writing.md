# Survey Writing

Use this when the active paper mode is `survey`.

## Core Survey Workflow

1. Clarify the survey story before polishing sentences.
2. Make the coverage boundary explicit:
   - what is in scope
   - what is out of scope
   - what is only background
3. Build comparison axes before drafting long prose.
4. Draft section-by-section with one paragraph = one message.
5. Reverse-outline every section after drafting.
6. Run skeptical self-review for:
   - coverage breadth
   - comparative depth
   - unsupported synthesis
   - contradiction handling
   - boundary honesty

## Non-Negotiable Survey Principles

1. Do not turn the manuscript into a bibliography dump.
2. Every major section should synthesize, not just summarize.
3. Comparison is core content:
   - compare method families
   - compare assumptions
   - compare benchmarks / metrics
   - compare strengths / failure modes
4. Keep explicit blind spots and omitted directions visible.
5. If two papers are not directly comparable, say so instead of forcing a ranking.
6. If the survey packet is thin, narrow the claim or request more coverage instead of faking confidence.

## Minimum Comparative Content

At least once in the survey body, make these explicit:

- family-to-family comparison
- benchmark / dataset comparison
- backbone / modality comparison when relevant
- contradiction or disagreement note
- tradeoff paragraph

## Section Guidance

- `Introduction`: explain why the topic matters now and what the survey contributes beyond a reading list.
- `Scope and Protocol`: make inclusion, exclusion, search boundary, and blind spots explicit.
- `Taxonomy`: define families with clean boundaries and note where boundaries blur.
- `Evidence Synthesis`: compare representative methods under shared axes, not one paper at a time.
- `Benchmark Landscape`: state which comparisons are fair, unfair, or incomplete.
- `Open Problems`: tie future directions to evidence gaps rather than generic future-work filler.

## Final Check

Before calling the draft stable, answer:

1. Does each section contain synthesis rather than only summaries?
2. Are the comparison axes explicit enough that a reviewer can follow them?
3. Are blind spots and non-comparable results stated honestly?
4. Are major claims in the survey backed by concrete included papers or matrix evidence?
5. Would an external reader learn the field structure, not just the paper list?
